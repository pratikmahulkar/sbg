ACP – Aluminium Composite Panel

Aluminium Composite Panel or ACP sheet is a new-age material used for building front elevation (facades), interiors, signage, modular kitchens and much more. It is available in a wide range of colours and textures like wood, stone, sand, 3D etc. It can be bent, folded and turned into shapes that can't be achieved by any other material. Hence, ACP sheet allows more design liberty and is thus a designer's delight.

Available in all sizes from 3mm to 10mm (on demand)

Louvers

The Louver Aluminium Section comprises a series of two rows of blades, both horizontal and vertical, that interlink with each other and can be moved according to the desired pattern. Smooth functioning and unparalleled quality are some of the unique features of Louvers.

There are four basic types of louvers:

Stationary louvers
Equipped with angled or airfoil-style blades that may provide weather protection and sound attenuation.
Adjustable louvers
With airfoil blades, these are also commonly available.
Combination louvers
Have blades that begin to open at one level of static pressure and become fully open at another. They may have backdraft damper blades and feature an all-aluminium construction for corrosion resistance.
Air measuring louvers
Have closely spaced vertical blades that prevent ingress of water.

Ventilated Facades

Ventilated facades can improve a building's protection against noise pollution from outside. In this system the air cavity is almost totally open, fully ventilated and not very wide. Ventilated facade systems are increasingly common as they are light, easy to install and have the above advantages in new and renovated buildings.

The system improves protection from direct sun radiation through the double envelope and the ventilated air cavity that dissipates heat. It also reduces the transfer of humidity from rain to the building, as the ventilated air cavity dissipates excess humidity.

Cementitious Facades

Cementitious facades are Calcium Silicate Board made from siliceous and calcareous materials reinforced with cellulose fibres. The boards are made using a laminar process and then an HPSC process to give a stable crystalline structure.

Cementitious facades score overwhelmingly against plywood, gypsum, MDF boards or any other wood composite boards in the market, and are the ideal choice for dry construction – be it walls, partitions or ceilings.

Some of the most remarkable properties

  • Fire resistant
  • Water resistant
  • Termite resistant
  • Fungus resistant
  • Sound resistant
  • Thermal insulation
  • Eco-friendly
  • Superior finish

Spider Glass

Glass, being an extremely versatile material, has found a number of uses in our day-to-day lives. Thanks to advancements in technology, glass and glass products are used indoors as well as outdoors. Glass is extensively used to make windows, doors and other supporting structures. Amidst it all, spider glazing installed with glass spider fittings has come up as an effective solution for exterior bolted glass assemblies.

It provides support to high-end exterior glass structures by means of point fixings. Constructed out of stainless steel, these fixings are excellent for absorbing and distributing all dynamic and static loads of the glass structure, such as the dead weight of the glass, differential expansion due to temperature fluctuations, and wind loading. Apart from fixings, the system also includes fasteners, glass and spider brackets.

Acoustic Walls

An acoustic panel is a sound-absorbing panel used to mitigate background noise and reduce reverberation and echo in a space. We refer to acoustic panels in their broadest sense, including vertical and horizontal panels.

Materials used for acoustic walls

Soundproof drywall typically combines several layers of gypsum boards, along with steel, with the main aim of increasing its density and mass so as to block sound effectively. Any type of drywall can work well for soundproofing as long as decoupling is kept in mind.

Many other products are used as per site requirements or specifications by authorities.

Acoustic Ceilings

Acoustic ceiling panels serve as barriers to block sound from travelling to adjacent rooms and also reduce noise within a room. As they soften, remove and diffuse sounds, acoustic panels help create a more pleasant environment, free of echoes and bothersome noise.

Materials used for acoustic ceilings

Most ceiling panels are made from mineral fibre, but other materials such as fibreglass are available. Mineral fibre panels typically have a high Noise Reduction Coefficient (NRC), which means they are good at absorbing sound within a space to prevent echo or loud environments.

A combination of rock wool and glass wool material, and tiles made from it, is also used.
